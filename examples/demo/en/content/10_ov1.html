This section teaches no models. It does one thing: turns "uncertainty" into computable symbols. The Naive Bayes classifier and logistic regression that follow are built entirely on the notation and the two rules defined here — so these 8 pages deserve a slow read. Skip them and you will keep stumbling over expressions like .

The through-line of these 8 pages
  1. Define the task (P2–P3): supervised learning is "predict from input "; continuous is regression, discrete is classification.
  2. Define the language (P5–P6): three quantities — joint, marginal, conditional probability — plus the product rule and the sum rule.
  3. Assemble one tool (P6–P7): Bayes' theorem — "posterior ∝ prior × likelihood" — the complete mechanism for updating beliefs with evidence.
  4. Hand out a map (P8): every probabilistic model must answer representation / inference / learning.
